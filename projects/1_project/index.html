Student Mental Health and Well-Being Study

Project Overview

Principal Investigator: Joy Lee-Shi, MA, Department of Management and Social Sciences, University of Belize

Collaborator: Dr. First Last name

Status: Pre-data collection

Population: Tertiary-level students in Belize

Design: Online cross-sectional quantitative survey

Target sample: About 600 participants

Mental health and well-being are important concerns among tertiary-level students, who may experience academic, financial, social, and personal demands while pursuing their education. Despite growing international attention to student mental health, comparatively little research has examined these experiences within the Belizean tertiary education context.

This study examines perceived stress, symptoms of anxiety and depression, grit, and coping strategies among tertiary-level students in Belize. In addition to describing students’ experiences, the project will investigate how these factors relate to one another and whether patterns differ across groups of students.

A second major aim is to examine whether several widely used psychological measures demonstrate appropriate psychometric properties when used with tertiary-level students in Belize. The study will evaluate the reliability and factor structure of the:

  • Perceived Stress Scale (PSS-10)
  • Patient Health Questionnaire-4 (PHQ-4)
  • Short Grit Scale (Grit-S)
  • Brief COPE

Establishing evidence for how these measures function within the Belizean context may support future psychological research, program evaluation, and the development or selection of student mental health initiatives.

Research Aims

This project seeks to:

  1. Describe levels of perceived stress among tertiary-level students in Belize.
  2. Examine symptoms of anxiety and depression within this population.
  3. Examine students’ levels of grit and perseverance toward long-term goals.
  4. Identify the coping strategies students report using most frequently.
  5. Investigate relationships among perceived stress, psychological distress, grit, and coping.
  6. Evaluate the reliability and structural validity of the PSS-10, PHQ-4, Grit-S, and Brief COPE within the Belizean tertiary-level student population.

Participate in This Study

Are you currently enrolled at a tertiary-level institution in Belize?

You may be eligible to participate in this study.

Participation involves completing an online survey about stress, emotional well-being, grit, and coping. The survey also includes demographic questions that will help describe the experiences of tertiary-level students across Belize.

Participation is entirely voluntary. Before beginning the survey, you will be provided with detailed information about the study, including its purpose, procedures, potential risks and benefits, confidentiality protections, and your rights as a research participant. You can decide whether you wish to participate after reviewing this information.

After completing the survey, you will receive a personalized summary of selected scores related to stress, psychological well-being, grit, and coping. These results are provided for informational and self-reflection purposes only and do not constitute a clinical diagnosis.
